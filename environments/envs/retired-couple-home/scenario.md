# Scenario: 2401, the Nakamura house, with a furniture maker in the garage

The same building as the reference environment — the two-storey stucco box over four garage doors at "2401", 12.6 m wide and 9 m deep, garage at street grade, home above, entered from the side stairs behind the gate. Different people, a different plan, and a garage level that is not a garage any more. Everything below is fictional.

## 1. Who lives here

**The Nakamura household**, two people and a dog. They bought the house in 1979 for $92,000 on one machinist's wage and one librarian's. Prop 13 froze the tax bill and the mortgage burned off in 2004, which is the only reason two retired public servants still live four blocks from the beach.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **Walter Nakamura**, 76 | Retired machinist — twenty years in the aerospace shops off Jefferson, then twenty-two at a job shop on Colorado making one-off parts. Licensed ham since 1974, call sign K6WNK; talks to Japan on Tuesday nights from the little back room. Left knee is bone-on-bone and the replacement is booked for March, so there is a cane by the front door, a grab bar in the shower, and a chair he can get out of. Watches the Dodgers loud. Cannot walk past an open workshop door without leaning on the frame to watch. | pension + SS ~$46k |
| **Marisol Reyes-Nakamura**, 71 | Retired school librarian, thirty-one years with the district, the last nineteen at the middle school. Quilts — Ocean Park guild, Thursday mornings, four finished tops a year and a stack that will never be finished. Reads two novels a week, mostly library copies, and does the crossword in pen. Cooks for six out of habit even though they are two. Drives; Walter does not any more. | pension ~$54k |
| **Ziggy**, 12 | Beagle-terrier mix, deaf on the left, sleeps in the sun by the front window and eats beside the fridge. |

Household income about $100k, plus $1,500 a month from the garage. Married since 1974. Their daughter **Renata**, 44, is a nurse in Torrance; the grandchildren **Téa** (9) and **Sam** (6) stay one weekend a month in the little back room, which is why there is a daybed in with the radio gear and a toy box under the bookshelf.

**Their tenant.** **Inês Barbosa**, 38, furniture maker, trades as Barbosa & Co. Six years in a production shop in Gardena, on her own since 2021, commissions only: dining tables, chairs and casework in walnut and white oak. She rents all four bays for $1,500 a month on a one-year lease, works Tuesday to Saturday, and agreed in writing to no machines before nine or after half past five — the neighbours, and Walter's radio hours. The Camry lives on the driveway pad now; that was part of the deal. She made them the white oak bench in the entry and the little walnut side table beside Walter's chair, and knocked a month off the rent for the pair.

**Taste, and what it puts in the rooms.** Fifty years of not replacing things: a 1980s walnut dining set, a sofa from 2011, two chairs chosen for how easy they are to get out of, a wall of library-sale hardbacks, Marisol's quilts on every soft surface, framed photographs on any flat one. Nothing bought since 2016 except the television, which is too big.

**Daily life, and where it leaves traces.** They eat at the kitchen peninsula, so the dining table has had a 1,000-piece puzzle on it since Christmas. The kettle is always on the stove and a dish towel over the oven handle. The crossword and the pill bottles live on the walnut side table with the reading lamp. Ziggy's bed is in the window and his bowls are by the fridge. Marisol's machine is threaded and a quilt is on the frame in the sewing room; the iron is still out. Walter's logbook is open on the radio desk and his headphones are where he dropped them. Slippers on his side of the bed, cane by the door, laundry basket in the bath. Downstairs, the yellow line Inês taped on the concrete so Marisol can reach the washer on Tuesdays without walking through the shop.

## 2. The plan

Metres, origin at the centre of the footprint, +z toward the street. Interior clear space 12.1 by 8.5 m; exterior walls 0.25 m, interior 0.12 m, cut at 0.8 and 0.85 m for the dollhouse view. Front door on the right-hand side wall at the top of the exterior stairs, so there is no interior stair and the garage is a separate model. The four front windows are placed where the exterior model has them: x = −5.2 (0.70 m), −1.4 (0.95 m), +2.15 (2.35 m), +5.3 (1.35 m). The kitchen sink sits under the middle one and the living room takes the big three-pane window; sill markers on the cut wall show where each opening starts.

### Garage level, Barbosa & Co.

One open space, 12.1 by 8.5 m, concrete, four doors to the street. Read left to right as a furniture shop reads: stock, machines, bench, finish.

- **Bay 1** — stock: cantilever rack on the outside wall with walnut, oak and cherry in stick; two sheets of ply leaning on the back wall; the dust collector in the corner with its hose run along the floor to the saw; Inês's bike by the door; the broom and the day's sweepings.
- **Bay 2** — machines: cabinet saw with its extension wing and fence, bandsaw on the back wall, jointer along the outside wall, an offcut crate.
- **Bay 3** — bench: the joiner's bench in the open with a face vice, dog holes, a plane, chisels, a mallet, a mug and a drawer part-made; shavings on the floor; the rolling tool chest and the wall of clamps behind it; a tabletop in glue-up on sawhorses with five bar clamps across it; a shop stool, and the chair Walter sits in to watch.
- **Bay 4** — finish and the landlord's corner: the dust curtain pushed back on its track, the finishing table with cans, rags and a pair of nightstands in oil for Renata, shelves of finish, two chairs done and waiting; the house's washer, dryer, water heater and utility sink along the outside wall, and the yellow tape line from the fourth door to the laundry.

## 3. How it is built

Two scene models against the same runtime and catalog as the reference: `props/models/retired-couple-home-home.js` and `props/models/retired-couple-home-workshop.js`. Floors per room, walls from segments with door gaps and swung leaves, then catalog items placed by `(x, z, rotation)`. Everything the catalog does not have is composed inline from `api.box` and `api.cyl` in the same style: the television, the patchwork quilts, the sewing machine, the quilt frame, the ironing board, the level-entry shower and its bars, the window sill markers, the picture frames — and downstairs the cabinet saw, bandsaw, jointer, lumber rack, joiner's bench, glue-up with clamps, clamp wall, tool chest, dust collector, dust curtain, finishing table and the tape line. No prop was generated for this environment and nothing is embedded, so both scenes are self-contained.

## 4. What reads and what does not

**Reads.** The shop reads as a furniture shop at a glance: the clamp wall, the glue-up on horses and the bench in the open do the work, and the finished chairs by the curtain say what the machines are for. Upstairs the peninsula with two stools and the puzzle left on the dining table tell you two people live here and eat standing up; the quilt frame, the threaded machine and the ironing board make the sewing room unambiguous; the shower with its bars and no tub says who lives here better than any label. The yellow tape line is the one object that explains the whole arrangement — landlord and tenant sharing a floor.

**Does not.** The sill markers only hint at the front windows; from the dollhouse camera they read as a band on the wall top rather than as openings, and the back rooms have no windows at all because the exterior model has none on the back or sides. Thin things still vanish from this height — the cane, the shower riser, the clamp bars and the hand tools on the bench are lines rather than objects. The dust curtain reads as two bundles and a rail, not as a curtain. And with the walls cut at 0.8 m there is no sky in either still: the style survives on palette and lighting, not on the sunset.
